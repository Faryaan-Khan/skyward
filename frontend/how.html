Questions

Do you sell tickets?

No. Skyward finds and compares prices, then sends you to the airline or booking site, where you book and pay.

Why is the price a bit different on the booking site?

Fares change constantly. Ours is the cheapest real fare found recently; seats at that price can sell out. Click through to see the live price.

What does "predicted drop" mean?

The model expects that fare to get cheaper before departure, and shows the best day to buy. It's a forecast, not a guarantee, so if the price already suits you, book.

01 Where every price comes from

Skyward doesn't invent or estimate the prices you see in the fares table. Each one is a real fare that travellers recently found through a large flight-comparison network covering hundreds of airlines and travel agencies.

For every route you look at, Skyward asks for both the cheapest flights overall and the cheapest direct flights. That's why low-cost and full-service airlines appear side by side in "Compare airlines".

Busy routes are checked every couple of minutes, and everything else a few times an hour. Fares change constantly and seats sell out, so the booking site always has the final say.

02 How the forecast learns

The forecast is a machine-learning model trained on a large history of fares. It looks at the patterns that really move ticket prices:

  • How far ahead you book: fares tend to be high very early, dip, then climb sharply near departure.
  • Season: school holidays, year-end and summer are busier and pricier.
  • Festivals and holidays: Christmas, New Year, Chinese New Year, Hari Raya, Deepavali and more.
  • Day of the week: some departure days are consistently cheaper than others.
  • The route itself: short hops and long-haul flights behave differently.

Instead of memorising each route, the model learns how prices move compared with what's normal for that route. That's what lets it make a sensible forecast for a route it has only just started watching, anywhere in the world.

The model is retrained automatically many times a day as new prices arrive, so it keeps adapting to the real market. Its average error above is measured on prices it wasn't trained on.

03 BUY, WAIT and predicted drops

For your exact travel date, Skyward imagines buying the ticket today, tomorrow, next week… all the way to departure, and predicts the price for each of those days.

  • BUY: today is already the best (or close enough that waiting isn't worth the risk).
  • WAIT: the model expects a meaningfully lower price later. It shows you roughly how much and when.
  • Predicted drops: trips across Skyward where a clear fall is expected, biggest first.
  • Deals: the opposite. Real fares available right now that are well below what that route normally costs.

A forecast is a well-informed estimate, never a guarantee. If a price already works for you, book it.

04 How "Popular this week" is chosen

Every week Skyward blends two signals: what Skyward travellers searched over the past seven days and what's trending worldwide across major travel hubs. To keep the list interesting, no single destination is allowed to dominate it. Automatic page refreshes don't count as searches, so the list reflects real interest.

05 Our promise on alerts
  • Email alerts only start after you confirm your address, so nobody can sign you up.
  • Alerts are based on a real price you can buy, never on a forecast.
  • At most one message per trip per day, and a repeat only if the price falls further.
  • Every message has a one-tap stop link; on Telegram, just send /stop.
06 Security & reliability
  • Encrypted connections everywhere, strict browser security rules and protection against automated abuse.
  • No accounts, no tracking, and no payment details: you always pay on the airline's or agency's own site.
  • Your alerts are tied to a private key that stays in your browser.
  • Every update is automatically tested and security-scanned before it goes live.
07 What Skyward can't do (yet)
  • Show a seat-level live quote from every airline. Prices are recent real fares, so confirm on the booking site.
  • Cover every date on very quiet routes: if few people search a route, there may be gaps.
  • Return trips, multi-city and business class are on the way; today it's one-way economy for one adult.
